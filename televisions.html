Screen size decides most of what a TV costs to run

A test of every television registered for sale in Australia. Does a bigger screen, or a premium OLED screen, cost much more to run? Four findings, then a calculator for your own TV.

In this report

  1. Most TVs are 43 to 75 inches
  2. Bigger screens use more
  3. Large costs five times small
  4. Screen type matters less
  5. Who makes them
  6. Check your own TV

1

Most TVs on sale are 43 to 75 inches

Around two in three models have a screen between 43 and 75 inches, and the most common sizes are 55 and 65 inches. Very small and very large screens are much rarer.

2

Bigger screens use more electricity

Each dot is one model. As screen size goes up, the energy use on the label1 goes up with it, from under 100 kWh a year for the smallest sets to more than 1,000 kWh for the largest. The pattern holds across brands.

3

A large TV costs about five times as much to run as a small one

At an example price of $0.33 per kWh2, an average large TV costs roughly $250 a year to run, compared with about $50 for a small one. Over five years of ownership that gap is close to $1,000.

Choose the screen your room needs, not the biggest one that fits on the wall.

4

Screen type matters far less than size

OLED televisions are often assumed to be power hungry. Compared at the same size, they are not: medium and large OLED models use about the same or slightly less energy on average than LED models. The gap between sizes is much bigger than the gap between screen types.
